# Storyboard #60 — The Empire Where Three Worlds Met (The Ottoman Empire)

> **Art style (every prompt):** Cinematic concept art, historical digital painting, hyper-detailed, dramatic lighting, epic realism, oil painting style. Characters ignore the camera. Uncropped full frame, textless, no letterboxing.
> **Density rule:** narration ≈ 2,600 words → min = floor(2600/1000) × 30 = **60**. Natural rhythm drives actual count (109 rows).
> **Continuity notes:** @ID1 = Osman I, default = dark-wool frontier tunic and fur-trimmed coat, wrapped turban, throughout Chapter 1. @ID2 = Mehmed II, default = deep red-and-gold brocade kaftan and jeweled turban with aigrette, age 21, throughout Chapter 2. @ID3 = Suleiman I, default = gold-and-crimson silk kaftan and tall white imperial turban; restate **elderly, silver-white beard and mustache, deeper lines, weathered dignity** for late-reign rows (from row 50 onward within Chapter 3). @ID4 = Constantine XI, default = purple Byzantine imperial regalia and crown; restate **without his crown or regalia, in a plain soldier's mail and helm** for the death-in-battle row only. @ID5 = Sultan Bayezid II, default = deep-blue-and-gold brocade kaftan, throughout his appearances. @ID6 = Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, default = Western-style olive military tunic, distinct modern dress from the Ottoman-era sultans. Siege, war, and the Armenian Genocide are handled soberly and non-graphically — symbolic imagery only, never gore or violence shown directly. The Ottoman civilization and its rulers are portrayed with historical accuracy and dignity throughout, never as a stereotype.
